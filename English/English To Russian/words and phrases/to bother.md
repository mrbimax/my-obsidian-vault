# to bother
?
# Transcription:
- BrE /tə ˈbɒðə(r)/; AmE /tə ˈbɑːðər/
# Meaning:
- To annoy, disturb, or cause trouble to someone.
- To make someone feel worried or upset.
- To take the time or trouble to do something; often in questions or negatives.
# Usage:
- Common patterns: bother someone, bother about/with something, don't bother, sorry to bother you, can't be bothered.
- Neutral to informal; very common in everyday speech.
# Example:
- _Stop bothering your sister while she's studying._
- _Does the noise bother you?_
- _Don't bother cooking — we'll order pizza._
- _I can't be bothered to clean the kitchen today._
# Russian:
- Беспокоить, надоедать, мешать; волновать, тревожить.
- Утруждать себя, утруждаться. _Sorry to bother you_ — извините за беспокойство; _can't be bothered_ — лень, неохота.
